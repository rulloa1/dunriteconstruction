Program Overview

Company vehicles are essential to our work and represent significant risk if not operated safely. This Fleet Safety Program sets the standards for who may drive, how vehicles are maintained, the rules for safe driving, and what to do after an accident. It applies to anyone who operates a Company vehicle or who drives a personal vehicle for Company business.

This Program expands on the Company Vehicles policy in the Employee Handbook (Section 13) and the Fleet Management procedure in the SOP Manual (SOP 105). Where they overlap, the most protective requirement applies.

Important — Please Read

This is a draft template for general information only and is not legal advice. Requirements differ for non-commercial vehicles versus vehicles subject to U.S. DOT / FMCSA regulation (which can include CDL, medical certification, hours-of-service, and a DOT drug-and-alcohol program). Before adopting, confirm which Company vehicles are DOT-regulated and have this Program and your insurance requirements reviewed by qualified advisors. Bracketed items must be completed.

Section One

Driver Qualification & Authorization

Only employees authorized by the Company may operate Company vehicles. To be and remain authorized, a driver must:

  • Hold a valid driver's license appropriate to the vehicle (and a valid CDL where required).
  • Maintain an acceptable driving record as determined by the Company and its insurer.
  • Complete any required driver orientation or training.
  • Authorize the Company to review their Motor Vehicle Record (MVR) at hire and periodically.
You Must Report

Immediately notify your supervisor if your license is suspended, revoked, restricted, or expired, or if you are charged with or convicted of a serious traffic offense or DUI. Driving on an invalid license is prohibited.

MVR Review

The Company reviews driving records before assigning a driver and on a periodic basis. A record that does not meet Company or insurer standards may result in loss of driving privileges and reassignment or other action.

Before operating a Company vehicle, perform a quick walk-around and check:

Maintenance & Defects

  • Report any defect or warning light promptly; do not operate an unsafe vehicle.
  • Preventive maintenance is scheduled by mileage/hours and tracked by the Fleet Coordinator.
  • Keep vehicles clean inside and out; report damage immediately.

Section Three

Safe Driving Rules

  • Seatbelts always. Every occupant wears a seatbelt whenever the vehicle is in motion — no exceptions.
  • Obey traffic laws. Follow posted speed limits and all traffic laws; adjust speed for weather, traffic, and load.
  • Keep your distance. Maintain a safe following distance (at least 3–4 seconds; more for heavy loads, towing, and wet conditions).
  • Drive for Florida conditions. Slow down for rain, standing water, and reduced visibility; watch for sudden storms.
  • Back up safely. Use a spotter where possible; walk around the vehicle before backing.
  • Park safely. Set the parking brake, chock when needed, and secure the load before leaving the vehicle.
  • Secure loads. Properly secure tools, materials, and equipment; never exceed rated capacity.
  • No unauthorized passengers. Carry only authorized passengers; no riding in truck beds or on equipment.
  • Rest. Do not drive fatigued; take breaks on long drives.

Section Four

Distracted & Impaired Driving

Zero Tolerance

Texting while driving is prohibited. Driving under the influence of alcohol, drugs, or impairing medication is prohibited and is grounds for immediate termination.

Phones & Distraction

  • Do not text, email, or use apps while driving.
  • Handheld phone use while driving is prohibited; if a call is necessary, use a hands-free device or pull over safely to a legal stop.
  • Program navigation before you start driving — not while moving.
  • Avoid eating, grooming, or other distractions that take attention off the road.
Florida Law

Florida prohibits texting while driving, and handheld device use is banned in active school and work zones. The Company's hands-free expectation applies everywhere, all the time.

Section Five

DOT & CDL Requirements

Employees who operate vehicles subject to U.S. Department of Transportation / Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) regulations must meet additional requirements, which may include:

  • A valid Commercial Driver's License (CDL) of the proper class and endorsements for the vehicle and cargo.
  • A current DOT medical certificate (DOT physical) where required.
  • Compliance with hours-of-service rules and accurate logs/records as applicable.
  • Participation in the DOT drug-and-alcohol testing program (pre-employment, random, post-accident, reasonable suspicion, return-to-duty, follow-up).
  • Pre-trip and post-trip inspections and defect reporting for regulated vehicles.
Confirm Applicability

Whether a vehicle is DOT-regulated depends on factors such as weight rating, use, and whether it crosses state lines. The Company must identify which vehicles and drivers are covered and apply these requirements accordingly. Confirm with qualified transportation/compliance advisors.

Section Six

Accident Response

Report every accident involving a Company vehicle — or a personal vehicle used for Company business — immediately, no matter how minor.

At the Scene

  1. Stop & secure.Stop safely, turn on hazards, and check for injuries. Call 911 if anyone is hurt or if required by law.
  2. Do not admit fault.Be courteous but do not admit fault or discuss who was responsible.
  3. Exchange information.Exchange license, insurance, and contact information with other drivers; get witness names and numbers.
  4. Document the scene.Photograph vehicles, damage, positions, road/weather conditions, and the surrounding area if safe to do so.
  5. Notify the Company.Call your supervisor immediately; do not arrange repairs or accept settlement without Company authorization.
  6. Complete the report.Complete the Company Accident Report and cooperate with the insurer and any investigation. Post-accident drug/alcohol testing may apply.
Keep in the Vehicle

Keep an accident information packet (insurance card, accident report form, emergency contacts, and a disposable or phone camera) in each Company vehicle.
