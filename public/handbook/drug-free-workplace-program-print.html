Program Overview

DunRite Construction Group LLC is committed to a safe, healthy, and productive workplace. Because construction is safety-sensitive work, drug and alcohol use endangers everyone on the jobsite. This Drug-Free Workplace Program describes the substances we test for, the circumstances under which testing occurs, and the rights and responsibilities of employees and applicants.

This Program supplements the Drug & Alcohol Policy in the Employee Handbook (Section 10). It is designed to be consistent with Florida's Drug-Free Workplace Program statute so the Company may qualify for available workers' compensation premium credits and related benefits.

Important — Please Read

This is a draft template for general information only and is not legal advice. Florida's Drug-Free Workplace Program (Fla. Stat. § 440.102 and Fla. Admin. Code) imposes specific notice, testing, laboratory, confirmation, MRO, and confidentiality requirements. Before adopting, DunRite must have this Program reviewed by qualified counsel and its workers' compensation carrier, select a certified laboratory and Medical Review Officer (MRO), and provide the required 60-day advance notice before testing begins. Section One

Policy & Prohibited Conduct

It is DunRite's policy that employees and applicants are free of the influence of drugs and alcohol while performing Company work. The following are strictly prohibited and may result in termination:

  • The unlawful use, possession, sale, distribution, or manufacture of illegal drugs or controlled substances.
  • Being under the influence of alcohol or drugs while on duty, on Company property, or on a jobsite.
  • The use of alcohol during work hours, including meal and break periods, while on duty.
  • The misuse of legal drugs (including prescription and over-the-counter medication) in a way that impairs safe job performance.
  • Refusing to submit to a required test, or tampering with, adulterating, or substituting a sample.
Prescription & OTC Medication

If you are taking a medication that may affect your ability to work safely, notify your supervisor before performing safety-sensitive duties. You are not required to disclose the underlying medical condition. The Company will not take action based solely on the lawful, as-directed use of a valid prescription, but you remain responsible for working safely.

Florida Medical Use

Florida law on medical marijuana does not require employers to accommodate on-the-job use or impairment, and marijuana remains federally illegal. Confirm the Company's position on lawful off-duty use with counsel and reflect it here.

Section Three

Substances Tested & Testing Procedures

Substances Tested

Testing covers alcohol and the controlled substances specified under the applicable testing standard, which commonly include marijuana/THC metabolites, cocaine, amphetamines, opioids, and phencyclidine (PCP) and others as designated. The final panel is set by the Company's testing provider in accordance with Florida requirements.

How Testing Is Conducted

  1. Collection.Samples are collected by a qualified collector following chain-of-custody procedures that protect sample integrity and privacy.
  2. Laboratory analysis.Samples are analyzed by a licensed/certified laboratory using an initial screen.
  3. Confirmation testing.Any positive screen is confirmed by a second, more specific method before being reported as positive.
  4. Medical Review Officer (MRO).A qualified MRO reviews confirmed positives, contacts the employee, and considers legitimate medical explanations before reporting a verified result.
  5. Result & action.Verified results are reported confidentially to the Company's designated representative, who determines the appropriate action.
Refusals & Tampering

Refusing to test, failing to appear, or tampering with a sample is treated as a positive result and may lead to withdrawal of an offer or termination.

Section Four

Employee Rights & Confidentiality

Under Florida's Drug-Free Workplace Program, employees and applicants have certain rights, including:

  • Advance notice. The Company provides general notice of this Program before testing begins, as required by law.
  • Notice of a positive result. An employee who tests positive is notified and may request a copy of the test results.
  • Right to explain. The employee may, within the time allowed, explain or contest a positive result, including identifying prescriptions or over-the-counter medications taken.
  • Confidential medication list. The employee may confidentially provide a list of medications to the MRO.
  • Challenge / retest. The employee may have the sample retested at a licensed laboratory, consistent with program rules and applicable law.
Confidentiality

All test results and related medical information are kept confidential, maintained separately from personnel files, and released only as permitted or required by law. Supervisors receive only the information needed to act on a result.

Section Five

Consequences & Assistance

Consequences of a Violation

A verified positive test, a refusal to test, or other violation of this Program may result in withdrawal of a conditional job offer (applicants) or discipline up to and including termination (employees), consistent with the Employee Handbook and applicable law.

Florida — Workers' Compensation

Under Florida law, an employee who tests positive for drugs or alcohol following a workplace injury may forfeit eligibility for workers' compensation indemnity benefits, subject to the statute's rebuttable presumptions and procedures. This is one reason prompt, lawful post-accident testing matters.

Employee Assistance

The Company can refer employees to an Employee Assistance Program (EAP) or community resources for substance-abuse concerns. Employees are encouraged to seek help voluntarily before a positive test or incident. Seeking help on your own initiative will not, by itself, be used as the basis for discipline, though it does not excuse policy violations or poor performance.

If you have a substance-abuse concern, talk to the office manager confidentially about available resources.
